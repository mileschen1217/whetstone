# 0012 — A row on the ship page is a block, not a table row

2026-09-24. Accepted.

**Context.** Since 0.1.0 each row of the ship page that is not green was a row of a five-column table, the Text column holding the criterion, finding or decision copied whole (0008). Three real readings of that table, all by the owner and kept as private material with their counts in `evals/BASELINE.md`: on the first real project's second unit the Text column was cut to one sentence at the owner's request; on this plugin's unit `line` the owner decided from the page and then said the raw text was hard for a person to read and the columns had no layout; on unit `boundary`, reading five decision rows, the owner for a moment did not know what to decide, because a criterion is a statement, not a question. On unit `read-before-signing` a FAIL row told the owner, who does not read the criteria table, nothing about which of the decisions he signed had gone red. Three shapes were proposed and two rejected: blocks above the line and the table below it (one kind of item in two shapes, so two parsers and two sets of graders); the table kept as the record with a second view generated for a person (a second artifact to keep in step and one more script); every row a block.

**Decision.** Every row on the ship page, above and below the line, is one block: a heading with the item and its follow-up tag, then fixed labelled lines. A verdict row leads with the decision it serves, copied through the criterion's `From` ids from the brief's `B-n` or the epic's `REQ-n`, then the evidence, the options with the recommended one first, and the criterion copied whole last. A finding, a builder's decision and a memory statement lead with their own line and carry no criterion. The labels are one fixed set in the owner's language; the page copies and does not paraphrase, so the raw text an agent acts on is still on the page (0008 holds). This is the item shape the epic and brief pages already have: a title, then sub-lines.

**Overturned by.** A real reading in which the owner cannot tell from a verdict block which signed decision went red (an `AC-n: no` line in the owner's reading record of a shipped unit); or a ship page whose blocks are longer than the table was for the same rows, in three consecutive real units.
